# Community Stewardship Support — Page Spec

**Version:** 1.0 — April 2026
**Companion to:** hylo-homepage-spec.md, hylo-get-involved-page-spec.md
**Purpose:** A page that honestly communicates what Hylo's stewardship support program offers, what it costs, and why it exists — without resorting to urgency tactics, inflated claims, or the apologetic tone that nonprofits sometimes adopt when asking for money in exchange for real work.

---

## The Tone Problem This Page Has to Solve

Most sales pages manipulate. Countdown timers, "only 3 spots left," testimonial walls, "what's it worth to you" anchoring. Hylo can't do any of that without betraying its values.

But the opposite failure is just as real: nonprofits and commons-based projects often undersell their services out of discomfort with exchange, burying pricing, hedging every claim, and framing payment as "donation" even when it's payment for labor. That's not integrity — it's avoidance. And it signals to prospective partners that the organization doesn't value its own work.

This page should be direct about three things:

1. **Community stewardship is skilled work.** It's not just "being nice online." It requires facilitation, strategy, cultural design, and sustained attention. Most groups underinvest in it and wonder why their community is dead.
2. **Hylo's team has spent years learning what works.** This isn't generic advice — it's hard-won knowledge from working with hundreds of groups across wildly different contexts.
3. **The money sustains the commons.** Every dollar goes to maintaining an open-source, non-profit platform. Paying for support is not a transaction — it's participation in the sustainability of shared infrastructure.

The voice should be: confident, warm, specific, honest about what you get and what it costs. No apologies. No hype. No scarcity games.

### Offering Layout

Four offerings presented as stacked cards (not side-by-side pricing columns — those invite comparison-shopping psychology). Each card is full content width, white background on the Athens Gray section below, with a left accent border.

*Design note: Stacking instead of columning is a deliberate choice. Side-by-side pricing grids are a conversion optimization pattern designed to anchor the middle tier. Stacking respects the reader's intelligence — each offering stands on its own, and the reader chooses based on what they need, not where their eye is steered.*

Each card: 4px border-radius. 32px padding. Left border: 4px solid, color varies by tier. Subtle shadow on hover (`0px 4px 15px rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.06)`). Background: White.

Card contents:
- **Tier name** — Onest Bold, 22px, Rhino. Left-aligned.
- **Price** — Onest Medium, 18px, Caribbean Green. Right-aligned on same line as tier name (or below on mobile).
- **One-line description** — Onest Regular, 15px, Rhino at 60%. Italic. 8px below name/price line.
- **What you get** — Onest Regular, 15px, Rhino at 75%. List of deliverables. Each preceded by a small Caribbean Green checkmark (12px). 16px below description. 8px between items.
- **Who this is for** — Onest Regular, 14px, Rhino at 55%. A single sentence in a slightly muted style. 20px below deliverables.
- **CTA** — "Start a conversation" — text-button, Caribbean Green, 14px. Links to email or form. 16px below "who this is for."

32px gap between cards.

### What This Page Does NOT Do

- **No countdown timers or artificial scarcity.** The offerings are available. People can take their time deciding.
- **No "what's it worth to you" anchoring.** We don't compare the price to "what you'd pay a full-time community manager" or "the cost of a dead community." The value is stated plainly and the reader judges for themselves.
- **No testimonial walls.** If strong, specific testimonials exist from past partners (Planetary Health Alliance, Prosocial World, etc.), one or two can be added to the offerings section — but only if they describe concrete outcomes, not vague praise. "Hylo's team helped us increase active participation by 40% in three months" is useful. "Working with Hylo was a delight!" is not.
- **No "most popular" badge.** Each offering serves a different need at a different scale. There's no default — the right choice depends on the group.
- **No urgency language.** No "limited spots," no "book before June," no "prices increase soon." The work is available. The page respects the reader's timeline.

### What This Page DOES Do

- **Names the problem directly.** Most groups underinvest in stewardship and wonder why their community dies. Saying this plainly is not manipulative — it's honest, and it's the reason this program exists.
- **States prices clearly.** No "contact us for pricing" on the first four tiers. The reader can evaluate whether this fits their budget without a sales conversation. Only Flourish (which is genuinely custom-scoped) omits a fixed price.
- **Explains where the money goes.** This is unusual for a sales page and it should be. Hylo is a non-profit commons — transparency about revenue is a competitive advantage, not a vulnerability.
- **Makes the CTA a conversation, not a checkout.** "Get in touch" rather than "Buy now." Every partnership starts with understanding the group's situation. This isn't a SaaS upsell funnel — it's a relationship.

### Testimonial Placement (if available)

If the team has strong, outcome-specific testimonials from past stewardship support partners, place one between Section 4 (Offerings) and Section 5 (How the Money Works). Format:

> "Quote about a specific outcome — what changed in their community because of the support."
> — Name, Role, Organization

Onest Regular italic, 18px, Rhino at 75%. Centered, max-width 560px. Attribution: 14px, Caribbean Green. Background: a subtle warm cream band or a thin-bordered card.

Only include if the quote is specific and outcome-oriented. No generic praise.

## Open Questions

1. **Intake form vs. email:** A short form ("Your name, organization, group size, what you're looking for, budget range") would make initial conversations more efficient. Is the team ready to build and maintain one, or is hello@hylo.com sufficient for now?
2. **Flourish pricing:** "Custom — reach out to discuss" is fine for now, but if the team has a typical range (e.g., $3,000–$5,000/month), stating it would reduce tire-kicking and set expectations. People comfortable with that range will reach out; people who aren't will self-select out.
3. **Pollination as prerequisite for Blossom:** This is stated in the current internal doc. It should be visible on the page so organizations don't plan for Blossom pricing alone and then discover there's a $2,500 prerequisite. The spec includes this in the prerequisites note.
4. **Naming:** "Pollination," "Seed," "Blossom," "Flourish" are evocative but they require the reader to decode which is which every time. Consider whether plain names ("Onboarding," "Basic Support," "Deep Support," "Dedicated Management") would serve better — or whether the nature metaphor is important enough to the brand to keep. The spec uses both: the nature name as the tier name, with a plain-language description immediately after.
5. **Team capacity:** Does the team currently have capacity to take on new Blossom or Flourish partners? If not, the page should say so honestly rather than collecting inquiries it can't serve. "We're currently at capacity for deep support partnerships. Join the waitlist to be notified when a spot opens" is more honest than silence.
6. **Past partner outcomes:** The strongest thing this page could have — and currently doesn't — is specific, measurable outcomes from past partnerships. "Planetary Health Alliance saw X% increase in cross-group activity after Pollination." "Prosocial World's onboarding completion rate went from X to Y." If this data exists, it transforms the page from "trust us, we're good at this" to "here's the evidence." Worth pursuing even if it takes a few weeks to gather.
